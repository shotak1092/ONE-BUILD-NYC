## LED or LCD: decide this first

A video wall is a group of displays, or modular LED cabinets, run as one image. In homes, lobbies, showrooms and offices the real choice is between tiled LCD panels and direct-view LED.

**LCD video walls** tile several thin-bezel panels. Samsung says its latest LCD video wall displays have [bezels that all but eliminate seams](https://www.samsung.com/us/business/displays/lcd-videowalls/explore/), but up close you can still see thin lines between panels.

**Direct-view LED walls** are built from modular cabinets where the diodes are the pixels, so the image is seamless and the wall can be built to custom sizes.

Watch the naming: most products sold as "LED TVs" or "LED displays" are LCD panels with LED backlighting. Ask whether a product is direct-view LED or a backlit LCD, because installation and price are very different.

| Factor | LCD video wall | Direct-view LED wall |
|---|---|---|
| Image | Thin bezel lines between panels | Seamless |
| Bright rooms | Fine for most interiors | Holds up in high ambient light |
| Shape | Rectangular grid | Custom sizes |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Lifespan | Shorter | Longer |

Christie Digital, a display manufacturer, sums up the trade: LCD's big advantage is [a low initial price](https://www.christiedigital.com/spotlight/choosing-video-wall-technology/), while LED walls stay readable in bright rooms, hold color uniformity and last longer.

## Mounting methods and when each fits

The right mount depends on what's behind the wall, how big the display is and how often someone needs service access.

- **Fixed wall mount.** The most common indoors. A frame or rail system anchors to studs, blocking or masonry, and the panels or cabinets attach to it. Living rooms, media rooms, lobbies, reception walls.
- **Recessed or built-in.** The display sits in a framed niche flush with the finished wall. Cleanest look, but it needs ventilation, a service plan and rough-in before the wall closes.
- **Suspended.** The display hangs from the ceiling structure. This takes real rigging knowledge and a ceiling that can carry the full load.
- **Floor-standing.** A base frame with rear bracing. Event spaces, stages and semi-permanent setups.

New York buildings add their own problems. Prewar plaster over masonry, steel-stud office partitions and party walls all take different anchors. A wall that looks solid can be hollow, or hide a riser or a neighbor's plumbing. That's why the survey comes before the product order.

## Prepare the site before the screens arrive

The screens are the easy part. The survey is where the install is won or lost.

### Structure and load

A video wall is heavy, and the weight sits on a small footprint. Confirm what's behind the finish: studs, blocking, brick, concrete or a hollow cavity. If the wall needs reinforcement, add blocking while it's open. During a [gut renovation](/services/gut-renovations/) that costs very little. On a finished wall it means demolition and patching. Hung or very large walls, and anything in a public space, should get a structural review by a licensed professional.

### Viewing distance and light

The closer people sit, the finer the pixel pitch you need. Mark where people will sit or stand, and check glare from the windows. In a Manhattan apartment with big south-facing glass, a bright LED wall or well-placed motorized shades can solve a problem a dimmer screen can't. Shades and screens are often planned together; see our guide to [motorized shades installation in NYC](/blog/motorized-shades-installation-nyc/).

### Power

A large wall draws real current. Put it on dedicated circuits, with grounding and surge protection planned in. If your panel is already full or old, the video wall may be the project that exposes it. Our guide to [electrical panel upgrades in NYC](/blog/electrical-panel-upgrade-nyc/) covers the signs, permits and planning.

### Data and signal

Plan the path from every source (media server, streaming box, computer, cameras) to the processor, and from the processor to the display. Add network drops for control and monitoring. Leave a service loop and access to the equipment, because every wall needs service eventually. See [what to pre-wire before the drywall goes up](/blog/what-to-prewire-before-drywall/).

### Ventilation

Processors, power supplies and receiving cards make heat. A closed cabinet or a niche with no airflow shortens equipment life. Decide where the rack goes, how it's cooled and how someone gets to it.

## How a video wall is installed, step by step

The sequence is similar across brands; follow the manufacturer's manual for your model.

1. **Survey and design.** Measure, check structure, power and data, and settle on display type, size and processor.
2. **Approvals.** Submit the co-op or condo alteration package where required, and file permits for the electrical work.
3. **Rough-in.** Run conduit, circuits, signal and network cabling, add blocking or steel, and set up ventilation and the equipment location. All before drywall.
4. **Mounting frame.** Install rails or the support frame level and square. A frame off by a few millimeters shows up as visible gaps across the wall.
5. **Hang panels or cabinets.** Check each unit for damage and dead pixels, then mount in order, aligning edges as you go.
6. **Connect.** Wire power, data and signal in the order the manufacturer specifies, and label every run.
7. **Configure.** Set up the processor so the separate units act as one canvas: layout, resolution, input mapping and scaling.
8. **Calibrate and test.** Adjust brightness and color uniformity, check alignment, then run the wall for several hours on test content before handover.
9. **Hand over.** Walk through the controls, leave documentation and mark the service points.

If the wall is part of a media room, the same process folds into a [home theater installation](/services/home-theater/), with screens, audio and lighting planned together.

## Who does what

- **Structure:** a licensed professional reviews hung, heavy or public-facing installations.
- **Line-voltage power:** a NYC Licensed Master Electrician, under a DOB permit.
- **Low-voltage signal, control and networking:** a qualified low-voltage installer.
- **Finishes:** the carpenters and painters who close up around the screen.

On commercial jobs, cutting into ceilings and walls also touches life-safety systems: smoke detectors, sprinkler heads, access control and alarm wiring. Plan around them before you design a ceiling-hung display.

## NYC rules: permits, buildings and work hours

### DOB electrical permits

The Department of Buildings says [no installation or modification of an electrical system can be done without a permit and a Licensed Master Electrician](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/property-or-business-owner/project-requirements-owner-electrical.page). Installations of 1,000 KVA or more also need construction documents from a Registered Design Professional approved by DOB; a residential or office video wall is far below that. Electrical permits [last up to three years from issuance and can be renewed](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/industry/project-requirements-skilled-trades-electrical-n-site-req.page). Whether a particular low-voltage run needs its own filing depends on the job, so have your contractor confirm it for your scope.

### Co-op and condo approvals

In a co-op or condo, the alteration agreement matters as much as the city. Expect to submit scope drawings, the contractor's license and insurance certificates naming the building and managing agent. Many boards restrict work hours, elevator use and noise, and some require the building's engineer to review anything that cuts into structure. Start early: board review often takes longer than the install. Our guide to [the co-op and condo alteration process](/blog/nyc-co-op-condo-renovation-alteration-process/) walks through it.

### Work hours and logistics

A crate of LED cabinets or a boxed large-format panel has to come up a freight elevator or stairwell in a window the building allows. Delivery slots, floor protection and debris removal all affect the schedule, so plan them before install day.

## What drives the cost

Hardware price depends on technology, size and pixel pitch, and LCD walls generally cost less up front than direct-view LED. The costs owners underestimate sit outside the hardware:

- Structural reinforcement and finish carpentry
- New circuits and, sometimes, a panel upgrade
- Cabling, network gear and the processor
- Permits, building fees and insurance paperwork
- Delivery, hoisting and after-hours work
- Calibration, programming and training

## FAQ

### Do I need a permit to install a video wall in NYC?

For the power, yes. DOB requires a permit and a Licensed Master Electrician for any electrical installation or modification, so new circuits for the wall need one. Low-voltage cabling and mounting are handled differently, and a co-op or condo will also need to approve the work.

### Is LED or LCD better for a video wall?

Neither wins everywhere. LCD walls cost less up front and suit most interiors, with thin lines between panels. Direct-view LED gives a seamless image, handles bright rooms and custom sizes, and costs more. Match the technology to the room and viewing distance.

### Can a video wall go on any wall?

No. The wall has to carry the weight and allow for ventilation and service access. Drywall alone isn't enough: you need blocking, steel or masonry anchors, and large or hung walls may need a structural review.

### How long does a video wall installation take?

The mounting itself is the short part. Approvals, rough-in and calibration take most of the time, and in a renovation the schedule follows the construction sequence. We give a timeline after the walkthrough and building review.
