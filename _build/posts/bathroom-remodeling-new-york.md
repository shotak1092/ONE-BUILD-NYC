## Three different jobs called "bathroom remodel"

Knowing which one you're planning keeps quotes comparable.

**A refresh.** New vanity, toilet, faucets, lighting, paint and regrouting. The tub or shower stays, the walls stay, the plumbing stays where it is. Fastest, least invasive, and often no permit.

**A full remodel on the existing layout.** Demolition to the studs, new waterproofing, tile, tub or shower, vanity and fixtures, updated lighting, fan and outlets, and new supply lines. Fixtures go back where the old ones were, so drains don't move.

**A gut renovation with layout changes.** Walls move, the shower gets bigger, the tub becomes a walk-in, the vanity relocates. That means new drain and vent routing, often new framing and, in an apartment, the most scrutiny from your building. If the bathroom is part of a larger apartment overhaul, our [gut renovation service](/services/gut-renovations/) covers the whole scope under one contract.

A useful rule of thumb: if the work touches drain, waste or vent piping, the water supply, structure or ventilation, plan it as permitted work, not a repair.

## What a bathroom remodel costs in New York City

National price guides don't apply here. Licensed labor costs more, materials get carried up freight elevators and walk-up stairs, work hours are restricted, and buildings add requirements for insurance, floor protection and supervision.

Here's how our bathroom work prices out:

| Scope | Realistic budget |
|---|---|
| Gut remodel with basic finishes | At least $15,000 to $25,000 |
| Luxury bathroom (stone, custom glass, steam, heated floors, layout changes) | No real ceiling: finishes and scope decide it |

We give a firm number after a walkthrough, because the same footprint in a Park Slope brownstone and a Midtown co-op can price very differently.

### What moves the number

- **Layout changes.** Relocating a drain or shower means new rough plumbing, new framing and often a longer approval. This is the biggest swing factor.
- **Tile and stone.** Large-format porcelain, marble and mosaics cost more to buy and much more to install, because they need flatter substrates and slower setting.
- **Fixtures and valves.** A thermostatic shower system, a wall-hung toilet with an in-wall carrier or a freestanding tub changes both the material list and the plumbing behind the wall.
- **Building access.** Walk-up versus elevator, freight elevator reservations and restricted hours all change how many productive days you get each week.
- **What's hidden.** Deteriorated supply lines, corroded cast-iron drains and soft subfloors are common in prewar Manhattan and Brooklyn buildings and can't be priced until the wall is open.
- **Electrical and smart upgrades.** Heated floors, lighting control, a defogging mirror or a motorized shade add electrical and low-voltage work that is far cheaper to plan before the walls close.

If budget is the constraint, our guide to [bathroom remodels on a budget](/blog/bathroom-remodel-on-a-budget-nyc/) covers where to spend and what to skip.

### Hold a contingency and define exclusions

Most friction between owners and contractors comes from the unknown. Good scoping separates three things: the fixed base scope, pre-priced items that depend on what's found once the wall is open (a rotted subfloor, failed supply lines), and explicit exclusions. Keep a contingency for conditions nobody can see, and require any added work as a written change order with a price before it starts.

## Permits, inspections and licensed trades

This is where New York differs most from the rest of the country, and where unlicensed shortcuts cause the most expensive problems.

### Do you need a permit?

The Department of Buildings says [most kitchen and bathroom renovations require an ALT2 permit application](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/property-or-business-owner/renovating-kitchens-bathrooms.page), filed by a New York State licensed Professional Engineer or Registered Architect. The same page lists painting and plastering as minor work that may not need a permit, and notes that contractors installing cabinets or resurfacing floors must be licensed by the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection. Whether plans are needed depends on how complex the work is, so the scope you settle on early sets the path.

### Who is allowed to do the work

A general contractor can't self-perform plumbing or electrical work in New York City. The DOB states that [electrical installations and modifications require a permit and a Licensed Master Electrician](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/property-or-business-owner/project-requirements-owner-electrical.page), and that [limited plumbing alterations require a Licensed Master Plumber and a permit](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/property-or-business-owner/project-requirements-owner-plumbing.page). A bathroom touches both, plus carpentry, waterproofing and tile.

That's why contract structure matters. When the general contractor, plumber and electrician are separate companies, each has its own schedule, its own insurance certificate and its own idea of what "ready" means. Under one contract, rough-in, inspections and finishes happen in order. We explain the sequence in [the right order of trades in a bathroom renovation](/blog/bathroom-renovation-order-of-trades/), and you can read more about our [licensed plumbing](/services/plumbing/) and [electrical](/services/electrical/) work.

Check any contractor's license before you sign. We work under NYC Home Improvement Contractor License #621290, and any firm you hire should give you its number up front.

## Co-ops and condos: approvals, insurance and building rules

In a co-op or condo, the building is effectively a second client. Nothing starts until the board or managing agent approves the plans.

**The alteration agreement.** It sets what you can do, when, and under what insurance. Board review timing varies widely: Brick Underground reports [approvals that come through in weeks and others that take six months or longer](https://www.brickunderground.com/improve/urban-standard-gut-renovation-costs-timeline-condo-co-op-NYC). Have a contractor or architect read the alteration agreement before plans are drawn, so you don't pay for a design the building won't allow.

**Wet-over-dry rules.** Many buildings won't let a bathroom or kitchen move over a neighbor's bedroom or living room. A new shower location that makes sense in your layout can be a non-starter under the building's rules. Check before you commit to a floor plan.

**Insurance and fees.** Buildings typically require certificates of insurance naming the building and managing agent, and many charge alteration review or deposit fees. Ask the managing agent for the full list early.

**Work hours and access.** Most buildings limit noisy work to weekday daytime hours and require freight elevator bookings, floor and hallway protection, and debris removal by set routes. Get the building's rules in writing before the schedule is set.

Our guide to [the co-op and condo alteration process](/blog/nyc-co-op-condo-renovation-alteration-process/) goes deeper. For managing agents, the practical benefit of our setup is one contact and one insurance package for every trade on the job, instead of chasing certificates from three or four companies.

## How long a bathroom remodel takes

Most owners underestimate the time before demolition. The job runs in four phases:

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Design, selections and ordering | Layout, fixture and tile choices; everything ordered and delivered |
| Board and permit approval | Alteration agreement, plan review, insurance certificates, DOB filing |
| Construction | Demolition, rough plumbing and electrical, inspections, waterproofing, tile, fixtures |
| Closeout | Punch list, cleaning, sign-offs |

Construction itself is the shortest phase. In a co-op or condo, approvals are usually the longest. The cheapest way to protect the schedule is to finalize selections and have materials on site before the old bathroom comes out: a missing valve or backordered tile can stop work for weeks once the room is open. Leave room in the plan for city inspections and building conflicts.

## Planning checklist before you hire

- **Pick the scope.** Refresh, remodel on the existing layout, or gut with layout changes.
- **Read your alteration agreement.** Know the work hours, insurance requirements and wet-over-dry rules before you design.
- **Confirm licenses.** Get the contractor's home improvement license number and confirm a Licensed Master Plumber and Licensed Master Electrician are doing that work.
- **Lock selections early.** Tile, fixtures, valves and vanity chosen and ordered before demolition.
- **Get the scope in writing.** Base scope, condition-dependent items with prices, exclusions and the change-order process.
- **Plan the extras now.** Lighting control, heated floors, a motorized shade or a lit mirror are simple at rough-in and awkward afterward.
- **Hold a contingency.** Older buildings hide problems.

## FAQ

### How much does a bathroom remodel cost in New York City?

A gut remodel with basic finishes starts at about $15,000 to $25,000. Luxury bathrooms have no real ceiling; stone, custom glass, steam and layout changes drive the number. We price after a walkthrough rather than quoting sight unseen.

### Do I need a permit to remodel my bathroom in NYC?

Often, yes. The DOB says most kitchen and bathroom renovations need an ALT2 application filed by a PE or RA. Painting and plastering may not need a permit. Anything touching drains, vents or water supply should be treated as permitted work.

### How long does a bathroom renovation take in a co-op or condo?

Construction is the short part. Board review can take anywhere from a few weeks to six months or longer, so start the paperwork before you finalize anything else, and have materials on site before demolition.

### Can I move the toilet or shower in my apartment?

Sometimes. Many co-ops and condos enforce wet-over-dry rules that block moving a bathroom over a neighbor's bedroom or living area. Check your alteration agreement first, then have a contractor or architect confirm what's feasible.

### Who has to do the plumbing and electrical work?

Licensed professionals. NYC requires a Licensed Master Electrician for electrical installations and modifications, and a Licensed Master Plumber for permitted plumbing alterations.
