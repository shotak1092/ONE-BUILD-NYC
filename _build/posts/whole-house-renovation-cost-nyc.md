## Why national numbers don't carry over

NerdWallet's [2026 guide to renovation costs](https://www.nerdwallet.com/home-ownership/home-improvement/learn/cost-renovate-house) gives a national range of $15 to $150 per square foot, and says gutting a house to the studs and fully remodeling typically costs about $100,000 to $300,000. Those are national figures. They describe a suburban house with a driveway, a dumpster in the yard and a crew that can start at 7 AM and park out front.

An NYC renovation pays for things that range doesn't include: higher labor rates, licensed trades for plumbing and electrical, Department of Buildings filings, freight elevator bookings, protected hallways, limited work hours, and, in co-ops and condos, a board that reviews the plans before anyone picks up a hammer. Use the national number to understand scale, never to set an NYC budget.

## What NYC renovations cost per square foot

Brick Underground's [NYC cost guide](https://www.brickunderground.com/guides/how-to-renovate/cost-of-renovating-a-brownstone-vs-a-condo-or-co-op) gives these published averages:

| Scope | Published range per sq ft |
| --- | --- |
| Basic renovation, gut or non-gut | $100 to $200 (guts at the higher end) |
| Gut with mid-range materials | $250 to $300 |
| Higher-end work | $200 to $400 |
| Extreme cases | Up to $800 |

The same guide says the per-foot cost tends to fall in the same range for co-ops, condos and brownstones. What differs is scope: a brownstone usually needs more of it.

Its examples show how quickly totals climb:

- **2,500 sq ft prewar apartment, gut:** about $625,000 to $750,000, and roughly double that for top-of-the-line upgrades.
- **3,200 sq ft brownstone:** about $800,000 to $960,000 for mechanicals, kitchens, baths, flooring, roof, windows and facade. Major structural changes with top finishes push it toward $1.6 million, and a historic district closer to $2 million or more.

The guide is undated, so treat it as a frame, not a price list. Only a walkthrough of your home can turn a range into a number.

## Cosmetic, mid-range or gut: decide the scope first

"Whole house renovation" covers three very different jobs. Settle which one you're doing before you compare any prices.

- **Cosmetic refresh.** Paint, flooring, lighting fixtures, hardware, maybe new kitchen fronts and bathroom fixtures. Walls and systems stay. Lowest cost, fewest permits, shortest schedule.
- **Mid-range renovation.** New kitchen and bathrooms, some wall changes, new flooring throughout, targeted electrical and plumbing work. Most of the structure stays, but parts of the walls and floors open.
- **Full gut.** Everything comes down to the studs and subfloor. New plumbing, new wiring, new panel where needed, new low-voltage, new finishes. For the full gut picture, see our [gut renovation cost guide for NYC](/blog/cost-of-gut-renovation-nyc/).

What's behind the walls often decides for you. Galvanized or failing pipe, knob-and-tube or undersized wiring, or a panel that can't carry a modern kitchen can turn a "refresh" into a mid-range job fast. In older prewar buildings and brownstones, assume you'll find something.

## Where the money goes

A whole-house budget is the sum of its rooms and its systems.

**Kitchens and bathrooms.** These cost more per foot than any other room because they carry plumbing, electrical, ventilation, tile and cabinetry in a small space. On our own jobs, a bathroom gut remodel with basic finishes runs at least $15,000 to $25,000, and luxury bathrooms have no real ceiling. Kitchens vary even more with cabinetry, stone and appliances. Our guides to [custom kitchen remodels](/blog/custom-kitchen-remodel-nyc/) and [bathroom gut renovations](/blog/bathroom-gut-renovation-nyc/) go room by room.

**Electrical.** Many older NYC homes need more capacity before anything else can happen. A standard electrical panel replacement runs $1,500 to $3,000 on our jobs, and a 100 to 200 amp upgrade runs $3,000 to $5,000. In an apartment building, available service from the building's riser can limit what's possible, so check that early. See our [electrical panel upgrade guide](/blog/electrical-panel-upgrade-nyc/).

**Plumbing.** Keeping kitchens and bathrooms over existing drain lines is usually the biggest single saving in a whole-home job. Moving wet rooms means new rough plumbing, more floor work and often a harder board review.

**Low-voltage and smart home.** Networking, lighting control, motorized shades and audio cost far less to run during rough-in than to retrofit into finished plaster. See [what to wire and when](/blog/smart-home-wiring-nyc/).

**Hidden conditions.** The Department of Buildings notes that [pre-1987 buildings may have piping and equipment insulated with asbestos](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/dob/project-requirements-asbestos.page) and requires an asbestos assessment before a permit is issued. If abatement is needed, it becomes its own cost and its own step in the schedule.

## NYC costs that national guides leave out

### DOB filings and permits

Work beyond cosmetic usually needs a DOB filing, with plumbing and electrical done by licensed trades and inspected at the right stages. Permit, expediter and design fees sit outside the construction price. They vary by job, so get real figures for your building from your architect or expediter.

Since June 19, 2026, [city rules under Local Law 181 of 2025](https://rules.cityofnewyork.us/rule/home-improvement-contractor-disclosures/) require home improvement contractors to give you a written permit disclosure on or before signing: which permits are needed, who obtains them, what it takes to get them including estimated fees, and how you can check permit status in city databases. Read it before you sign.

### Co-op and condo requirements

In a co-op or condo, the building's alteration agreement controls scope, insurance, schedule and fees. Brick Underground lists typical lines:

- Upfront renovation fees of $250 to $500, or a deposit based on a percentage of the renovation cost
- Plan review by the board's architect at about $1,000 to $2,000
- Building architect supervision from start to finish at another $8,000 to $12,000 where required
- Contractor liability insurance requirements of $1 million to $5 million

Check your own alteration package for the exact figures and certificate wording. Our guide to [the co-op and condo alteration process](/blog/nyc-co-op-condo-renovation-alteration-process/) walks through each step.

### Work hours and access

The city [allows residential construction on weekdays, 7 AM to 6 PM](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01091), and many buildings set tighter limits. A walk-up with no elevator costs more to build in than a building with a bookable freight elevator. Fewer working hours stretch the schedule, which adds labor cost and your own carrying costs.

### Houses carry extra scope

A brownstone in [Brooklyn](/areas/brooklyn/) or a house in [Queens](/areas/queens/) or [Staten Island](/areas/staten-island/) can include roof, facade, windows, boiler and structural work that an apartment in [Manhattan](/areas/manhattan/) doesn't. In a landmarked district, exterior changes also go through Landmarks review, which adds time and design cost.

## How to build a budget that holds

1. **Define scope by room and by system.** List what you want in each room, then list what the building needs behind the walls: wiring, pipe, panel capacity, ventilation. The second list is where most surprises come from.
2. **Plan technology before the walls close.** Decide on lighting control, shades, audio and networking at the drawing stage.
3. **Get like-for-like quotes.** Have every contractor price the same drawings and allowances and list inclusions and exclusions in writing. Two quotes that look far apart often differ only in what they leave out.
4. **Hold a contingency.** Brick Underground advises budgeting at least 15 percent over the original estimate, plus a change-order line of 5 to 10 percent. Older buildings with more opened walls call for the larger end.
5. **Keep upgrades out of the contingency.** The reserve is for hidden conditions like a rotted joist or failing pipe. Finish upgrades chosen mid-project are change orders. Price them and decide them deliberately.
6. **Ask how the trades are sequenced.** Rough-in, inspection, close walls, finish. A quote that shows one schedule across all trades is easier to hold than four separate bids.

## FAQ

### How much does a whole house renovation cost in NYC?

Brick Underground's published NYC averages run from $100 to $200 per square foot for basic work to $250 to $300 for a gut with mid-range materials, and higher for high-end finishes or structural changes. Design fees, permits and building costs come on top. A walkthrough and written scope are the only reliable answer for your home.

### Is a whole house remodel cheaper than a gut renovation?

Usually, yes. A cosmetic or mid-range remodel keeps walls and systems in place, while a gut replaces them. Old wiring and plumbing often decide which route makes sense, so have the systems assessed before you settle the scope.

### How much contingency should I add?

Brick Underground recommends at least 15 percent over the original estimate, plus 5 to 10 percent for change orders. Plan for the top of that range in prewar buildings and brownstones, and keep the reserve for hidden conditions, not upgrades.

### Do permits and building approvals add to the cost?

Yes. DOB filings, design and expediter fees, and in co-ops and condos, board review fees, supervision fees, deposits and insurance requirements all add to the total. Your contractor must give you a written permit disclosure before you sign, so you know who is pulling what.

### Does a renovation cost more in a brownstone than an apartment?

Per square foot, published NYC averages are similar. Brownstones usually cost more in total because the scope is bigger: roof, facade, windows, heating and structure can all be part of the job, and landmarked blocks add review time.
