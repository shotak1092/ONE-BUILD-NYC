Blackout Bedroom Windows: How to Get a Truly Dark Room

How to blackout bedroom windows in NYC: compare shades and curtains, seal the edges, and plan around radiators, fire escapes and co-op rules.

To blackout a bedroom window, cover the glass with an opaque shade or curtain, then seal the edges, because the gaps around a window are usually what give the room away. The darkest setup is two layers: a blackout shade at the window and full-length curtains hung wide and high around it. In a New York apartment, with streetlights, neighbors' windows and early sun all working against you, mounting and layering matter more than the price of the fabric.

Why a dark bedroom is worth the effort

Darkness is a sleep signal. Stanford Lifestyle Medicine notes that darkness prompts the body to produce melatonin, and that light getting into the room can delay it. The same page warns that early-morning light coming through blinds can cause awakenings you're not even aware of.

NIOSH, the CDC's workplace health institute, says the same thing in its sleep training for shift workers: your bedroom should be very dark, and room-darkening shades or heavy, lined draperies can block the light coming through windows. It also says blinds can let too much light in, and recommends more opaque coverings instead.

That's where most people go wrong. They buy something labeled "room darkening," hang it tight to the glass, and still see a bright outline around it at 6 a.m.

The main ways to blackout a bedroom window

There are four families of solutions. Each trades off darkness, looks, effort and permanence.

Blackout shades

A blackout shade uses opaque fabric that stops light passing through the material. Roller, cellular and Roman shades all come in blackout versions. A shade covers the glass cleanly and stays out of the way when raised, which suits bedrooms with limited wall space.

On its own, a shade usually leaves a thin line of light at the sides, because it has to clear the frame or the wall. Popular Mechanics notes that shades mount inside or outside the frame, with outside mounting offering better light blocking, and that ceiling-mount shades work equally well.

Blackout curtains and drapery

Curtains do what shades struggle with: they cover the frame, the wall around it and the edges. Consumer Reports explains that some blackout curtains use multiple layers, with a woven polyester inner layer that makes a dense, stable fabric and a white color that reflects incoming light back out.

The weakness is geometry. A curtain hung tight to the window leaves light spilling over the top and down the sides. Hung properly, it solves that.

Blinds, and why they fall short

Blinds darken a room by tilting slats closed. Even fully closed, they let light through where the slats overlap and at the holes for the lift cords. Treat blinds as a privacy and glare tool, and let an opaque shade or curtain do the blackout work.

Temporary and renter-friendly options

Adhesive paper shades, suction-mounted panels and static-cling film all help when drilling isn't an option, such as in a short-term rental or while you wait for a permanent install. Test any adhesive on a small corner first, since removal can lift paint on older window frames.

For a bedroom you plan to live in for years, a fitted solution looks better, performs better and is easier to use every day.

Seal the edges: where the light actually gets in

Covering the glass is the easy part. The difference between a dim room and a dark one is what happens at the perimeter. Check five leak zones:

  • Top. Light spills over the rod or above the headrail. Mount higher, add a valance or boxed header, or use a ceiling-mounted track.
  • Sides. A gap between fabric and frame glows at dawn. Extend the rod well past the frame, or use wrap-around returns or side channels.
  • Middle. Two panels meet and a stripe of light shows. Overlap them generously, or use one wide panel.
  • Bottom. Light slips under a short hem. Choose floor-length panels that just touch the floor.
  • Fabric. The material itself is thin. Pick a true blackout fabric and check it against a lamp in the evening.

Hang curtains wide, high and full

Consumer Reports advises that curtains should cover the window entirely, with both the rod and the curtains extending well beyond the window's edges. In practice, that means a rod clearly above the trim, reaching several inches past each side of the frame, and panels wide enough to fold into deep pleats instead of hanging flat.

A panel that's barely as wide as the window will pull open at the edges and leak. Fullness isn't decoration here. It's the seal.

Use side channels for shades

The cleanest way to seal a shade is with side channels: tracks on each side of the window that the fabric runs inside, closing the gap at the edges. It's what we recommend when a client wants a bedroom as dark as it can get. Side channels work best when they're planned with the window opening and trim, not added over a finished surface.

Check for leaks when it counts

A room can look dark at 2 p.m. and still leak at dawn or from a streetlight. Stand in the room with the lights off at the time of day that bothers you most, and look at the edges, not the center. A bright lamp on the other side of the fabric will quickly show thin spots.

Layering: the most reliable setup

If you want the room as dark as it can get, use two layers. A blackout shade handles the glass. A full-length blackout curtain hangs outside the frame and handles the edges. The curtain covers the shade's side gaps, and the shade stops whatever gets past the curtain.

Layering has side benefits:

  • Flexibility. Close only the shade for an afternoon nap, or both for the night.
  • A softer room. Curtains add texture and hide the shade hardware.
  • Easier mornings. A motorized shade can open on a schedule while the curtains stay at the sides.

Choosing the right setup for your bedroom

A few questions narrow it down quickly:

  • How dark do you need it? A good blackout roller shade with curtains is plenty for most sleepers. Shift workers and light-sensitive sleepers should add side channels or a full layered setup.
  • What does the window look like? Deep recesses suit an inside-mounted shade with curtains outside. Shallow frames, or windows close to the ceiling, favor outside or ceiling mounts.
  • Who operates it? Tall windows and large panes are far easier with a motor than a cord.
  • Is it a rental or yours? Rentals call for no-drill options. If you own the apartment, a fitted install is worth it.
  • What does the building allow? Check with your managing agent before you order. Some buildings have rules on window changes and contractor access.

Most blackout shades include installation hardware, so settle the mounting position before you order.

Blackout in a New York bedroom

NYC bedrooms bring their own problems: a window facing a bright avenue in Manhattan, a Queens airshaft lit by a neighbor's kitchen, odd-sized openings in a Brooklyn brownstone. A few things to plan around:

  • Radiators. Floor-length curtains don't belong over a radiator under the sill. Let the shade carry the blackout work on those windows, and keep any curtain shorter or lighter.
  • Fire escape windows. FDNY says to keep your fire escape window clear and unobstructed. On that window, use a shade or curtain you can clear in a second, with nothing fixed across the opening. Side channels and fixed panels need extra thought here, so raise it before anything is ordered.
  • Out-of-square openings. Prewar window openings are rarely square. Custom-measured shades and side channels account for that. Off-the-shelf sizes leave gaps.
  • Building approvals. In co-ops and condos, wiring or alterations around a window may need board approval, an alteration agreement or an insurance certificate. Ask before you schedule anything. Our guide to the NYC co-op and condo alteration process explains how it works.
  • Work hours. Buildings limit when contractors can work, and the install has to fit those windows.

If you're already renovating, the best time to solve blackout is before the walls close. A power feed at the window head, a recessed pocket for the shade and a ceiling track for curtains are simple at framing and fussy afterward. See what to pre-wire before the drywall goes up.

Motorized blackout shades

A motorized blackout shade does the same job as a manual one and is easier to live with. You can close every shade in the room from a remote, a wall keypad or your phone, and set a schedule so the room is dark at bedtime and opens when you want to wake up. Tied into a smart home system and lighting control, the shades can join a "bedtime" scene that also dims the lights.

Battery shades need no wiring. Hardwired shades do: in NYC, connecting to household power takes a Licensed Master Electrician and a permit. Our write-up on motorized shades installation walks through the process from measurement to commissioning.

Mistakes that leave the room bright

  • Buying "room darkening" and expecting blackout. Room darkening reduces light. Blackout is designed to stop it. Read the product description closely.
  • Hanging the rod at the frame. Light spills over the top and past the sides.
  • Curtains that are too narrow or too short. Flat panels pull open, and short hems leave a bright stripe at the floor.
  • Ignoring the gap between panels. Overlap them or use one wide panel.
  • Skipping the evening check. Some leaks only show at certain hours, so test with the lights off and a lamp behind the fabric.
  • Choosing blinds for blackout. Slats and cord holes will always let some light through.

FAQ

What is the best way to darken bedroom windows?

Layer a blackout shade at the window with floor-length blackout curtains hung outside the frame. The shade covers the glass and the curtains cover the edges. If you only want one product, choose a blackout shade with side channels.

Why does light still come through my blackout curtains?

Usually the problem is the setup, not the fabric. Light slips over the rod, past the sides, between panels or under a short hem. Raising and widening the hardware often fixes it.

Are blinds good for blackout bedrooms?

Not on their own. Blinds leak light at the slats and cord holes, and NIOSH sleep guidance recommends more opaque window coverings. A blackout shade or lined curtain is the better base.

Should blackout shades be mounted inside or outside the window frame?

Outside mounting generally blocks more light because the shade overlaps the opening. Inside mounting looks tidier and works well when paired with curtains or side channels.

Can motorized blackout shades be installed in a co-op or condo?

Often yes, but you may need board approval, an alteration agreement or proof of contractor insurance, depending on the building and the scope. Battery shades are usually simpler than hardwired ones. Ask your managing agent first. We handle that paperwork and the work-hour limits as part of the project.
