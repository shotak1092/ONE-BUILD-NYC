Renovating in Brooklyn Heights

What the buildings here ask for.

Brooklyn Heights was designated New York City's first historic district in 1965. Its rowhouses, carriage houses and prewar co-ops mean almost every project involves either a co-op board, the Landmarks Preservation Commission, or both.

Many houses still carry old plumbing and wiring behind original plaster. We plan the trades so walls are opened once, systems are replaced while they're accessible, and original details stay intact.

Questions

Brooklyn Heights renovation questions.

How long does LPC approval add to a Brooklyn Heights renovation?

It varies by scope. LPC staff decide which permit type a job needs after reviewing the application, and simpler work moves faster. We file early so approval runs alongside design and the board review.

Can I add central air or a heat pump to a landmarked house?

Usually yes. Where the condensers and any new vents go is the landmark question. Equipment on a rear yard or roof, out of public view, is generally easier to approve than anything visible from the street.
