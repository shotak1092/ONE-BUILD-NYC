Renovating in Park Slope

What the buildings here ask for.

Park Slope is brownstone and limestone rowhouses, many inside the Park Slope Historic District, plus converted condos and co-ops. A lot of houses are being returned to single-family or reworked as an owner's duplex over a rental.

Old houses here usually need more than finishes: new plumbing risers, electrical service sized for modern kitchens and heat pumps, and floors that have settled over a century. We do the structure, the trades and the technology under one contract.

Questions

Park Slope renovation questions.

Do I need LPC approval to renovate a Park Slope brownstone?

If the house is in the historic district, any work that affects the exterior, and interior work that needs a DOB permit, requires a Landmarks Preservation Commission permit. We build that into the schedule.

Can I convert a two-family brownstone to a single-family home?

Often, but it changes the building's legal use, which means a DOB filing with drawings by a licensed architect or engineer and, in many cases, a new or amended certificate of occupancy. We coordinate that with your design professional.
